#### Risk identification
- An important part of any organization
	- Growth brings risk, we must be ahead of potential problems
- Risk maangement
	- Manage potential risk
	- qualify internal and external threats
#### Performing a risk assessment
- *One time*
	- Assessment may be part of a one-time project
	- e.g. Company acquisition, new equipment installation, unique new security threats, etc.
- *Continuous assessments*
	- A part of an existing process
	- Change control requires a risk assessment as part of the change
#### Ad hoc assessments
- An organization may not have a formal risk assessment process
- Perform an assessment when the situation requires
- CEO is back from a conference
	- Wants to know if the org is protected from a new attack type
	- Look at just this specific attack type and how it may apply
- Make a committee, assess the risk of that specific attack, then disband the committee
#### Recurring assessments
- Evaluation occurs on standards intervals
- e.g. An internal assessment
	- Every three most, do an internal check, and log findings.
- A mandated risk assessment
	- Required by certain organizations
	- Some legal requirements will mandate an assessment

#### Quantitative risk assessment
- ARO (Annualized Rate of Occurence)
	- How likely is that a hurricane will hit? In Montana? In Florida?
- Asset value (AV)
	- Value of the asset to the organization
	- Includes the cost of the asset, effect on company sales, potential regulatory fines, etc.
- Exposure factor (EF)
	- The percentage of the value lost due to an incident
- SLE (Single Loss Expentancy)
	- AV x EF
	- Laptop stolen = $1000 (AV) x 1.0 (EF) = $1000 SLE
	-  Whar is the monetary loss if a single event occurs?
- ALE (Annualized Loss Expectancy)
	- ARO x SLE
	- Seven laptops stolen a year (ARO) x $1000 (SLE) = $7000 ALE
- The business impact can be more than monetary
	- Quantitative vs qualitative
#### Impact
- Life
	- The most important consideration
- Property
	- The risk to buildings and assets
- Safety
	- Some enviornments are too dangerous to work
#### Likelihood and probability
- Risk likelihood
	- A qualitative measurement of risk
		- Rare, possible, certain, etc.
- Risk probability
	- A quantitative measurement of risk
	- A statistical measurement
- Often considered similar in scope, can be used interchangeably in casual conversation
#### Risk appetite and tolerance
- Risk appetite
	- A broad description of risk-taking deeming acceptable
	- Amount of accepted risk before taking any action to reduce that risk
- Risk tolerance
	- An acceptable variance from the risk appetite
- Examples:
	- Risk appetite
		- A highway's speed limit
		- An acceptance balance between safety and convenience
	- Risk tolerance
		- Ticketing usually occurs well above the posted limit
		- This tolerance can change with road conditions, weather, etc
#### Risk register
- Every project has a plan, but also has risk
	- Identify and document the risk associated with each step
	- Apply possible solutions to the identified risks
	- Monitor the results
- Key risk indicators
	- Identify risks that could impact the organization
- Risk owners
	- Each indicator is assigned to someone to manage that risk
- Risk threshold
	- The cost of mitigation is at least equal to the value gained by mitigation

## Risk Management Strategies
- Transfer
	- Move the risk to another party
	- Buy some cybersecurity insurance
- Accept
	- We'll take the risk! Often the usual course
- Accept with exemption
	- A security policy or regulation cannot be followed, so an exemption is required
	- Exemption may need approval
		- New device in company needs to not be patched, so approve an exemption excluding the device from monthly windows updates
- Accept with exception
	- Internal security policies are not applied
	- An exception is made to update timeframe of a patch
	- Exception = one time delay, etc.
	- Exemption = always a delay
- Avoid
	- Stop participating in a high-risk activity
- Mitigate
	- Decrease the risk level
	- Invest in security systems
#### Risk reporting
- A formal document
	- Identifies risks
	- Detailed information for each risk
- Usually created for senior management
- Common includes critical and emerging risks

## Business Impact Analysis
#### Recovery
- Recovery time objective (RTO)
	- Get up and running quickly
	- Get back to a particular service level
	- How long did it take to get essential systems back up and running?
- Recovery point objective (RPO)
	- How much data loss is acceptable?
	- Bring the system back online; how far back does data go?
	- The database is up, but only provides the last 12 months of data (RPO)
- Mean time to repair (MTTR)
	- Average time required to fix an issue
	- Includes time spent diagnosing the problem
	- An important metric for determining the cost and time associated with unplanned outages
- Mean time between failures (MTBF)
	- Time between outages
	- Can be used as a prediction or calculated based on historical performance
		- Total uptime / number of breakdowns
