The CIA Triad is a combination of principles and the fundamentals of security.
### Confidentiality
Prevent disclosure of info to unauthorized individuals/systems
- Encryption
- Access controls to selectivity restrict access to a resource
- 2FA
### Integrity
Messages can't be modified w/o detection. Data is stored and transferred as intended.
- Hashing
- Digital signatures (encrypt the hash asymmetrically)
	- Verifies integrity of data
- Certificates
	- Combine without digital signature to verify an individual
- Non-repudiation
	- Provides proof of integrity
### Availability
Systems & networks must be up and running 
- Redundancy
- Fault tolerance
- Patching
	- Stability, close security holes

## Non-Repudiation
You can't deny what you've said.

### Proof of Integrity
- Verify data does not change
- Use a hash, aka message digest or fingerprint
	- If the person changes, you get a different fingerprint
- Doesn't associate the data with an individual.
- If the hash is different, something has changed.

### Proof of Origin
- Prove the source of the message.
- Make sure the signature isn't fake (non-repudiation).
- The sender can encrypt the hash of the message with their private key. The receiver can use the public key of the sender to decrypt the signature. 
- The decrypted signature can be compared to the informations hash, if they match, then it's all good.
	- The data is original, and it must have been sent from the actual sender.

## Authentication, Authorization, and Accounting
**The AAA framework**

- Identification
	- This is who you claim to be
- Authentication
	- Prove you are who we claim to be
- Authorization
	- Based on your id and auth, what access do you have?
- Accounting
	- Login time, data sent/received, logout time

### Authenticating Systems
A system can't type a password, so how can we authenticate a device?
- Put a digitally signed certificate on the device.
- Other business processes rely on the cert
	- e.g. Access to the VPN from authorized devices
To do this, we will need a **Certificate Authority**.
##### Certificate Authority
- An org has a trusted Certificate Authority (CA)
- The org creates a certificate for a device, and digitally signs the cert with the organization's CA.
- The CA's digital signature is used to validate the certificate.

The org's CA was also signed by the **Root CA**. 
Device Cert -> CA -> Root CA

### Authorization Models
User or device now authenticated, now we need an authorization model.

**No auth model**
- Difficult to understand why an auth may exist
- Does not scale
- 10 people, each person needs different read/write/create perms for 5 resources. What about 1000 people?

**Add an abstraction**
- Reduce complexity. Make a clear relationship between user & resource.
- Add an employee to a group with dedicated permissions.
- Supports however many people need those perms.

## Zero Trust
A holistic approach to network security. You must authenticate yourself every time you use a resource. With zero trust, nothing is trusted, everything is subject to a security check such as 2FA, encryption, monitoring, etc.

### Planes of operation
- Split the network into functional planes
- **Data Plane**
	- Process the frames, packets, network data.
	- Processing, forwarding, encrypting, NAT
- **Control Plane**
	- Manages actions of data plane
	- Define policies and rules
	- Determine how packets should be forwarded.

### Controlling Trust
- Adaptive Identity
	- Consider the source and requested resources. Make authentication stronger if needed.
	- If source is from China, maybe do extra checks to determine who they are.
	- Other risk indicators: relationship to the org, type of connection, IP address, etc.
- Threat scope reduction.
	- Decrease number of entry points.
- Policy-driven access control.
	- Combine the adaptive identity with a predefined set of rules.

### Security Zones
Where are you coming from and where are you going?
- Trusted net to untrusted net
- Internal to external
- VPN1, VPN5, VPN11

Maybe have a rule that auto denies access if a device in an untrusted zone tries to communicate with a device in the trusted zone.

Some zones can be *implicitly trusted*, such as a trusted zone to an internal zone.

How can we enforce these policies?

### Policy Enforcement Point
- Subjects and systems.
- **Policy enforcement point (PEP)**
	- The gatekeeper
	- Can allow, monitor, and terminate connections
		- Can consist of multiple devices working together.
- **Policy Decision Point
	- The process for making an auth decision
- **Policy Engine
	- Evaluates each access decision based on policy
- **Policy Administrator**
	- Communicates with the PEP
	- Generates access tokens/credentials
	- Tells PEP to allow/disallow access

## Physical Security

#### Barricades / bollards
- Prevent access
- Channel people through a specific access point
	- e.g. Allow people, prevent cars and trucks
- Can be used to an extreme, like moats
#### Access control vestibules
- All doors normally unlocked
	- Opening one door causes other to lock.
- All doors normally locked
	- Unlocking one prevents others from being unlocked
- Promote *managed control* through an area. One at a time, in controlled groups.
#### Fencing
- Build a perimeter. Can be transparent or opaque.
- Should be robust and hard to climb.
#### Video Surveillance
- A network of CCTV cameras.
- Becoming more intelligent w/ motion detection, object detection.
#### Security guard & access badges
- Physical protection in an area to validate ID of employees.
- Two person integrity/control
- Access badge
	- Picture, name, other details
	- Electronically logged for doors
#### Lighting
- More light = more security
	- Attackers avoid light, easier to see for defenders
- Specialized design for lights
	- Lighting angles, avoid shadows/glare
#### Sensors
- Infrared for both light/dark areas
	- Common in motion detectors.
- Pressure
	- Detects a change in force.
	- Floor/window sensors
- Microwave
	- Detect movement over much larger area.
- Ultrasonic
	- Send ultrasonic waves and receive signal back.
	- Detect motion, collision detection, etc.

## Detection and Disruption

#### Honeypots
- Attract the bad guys & trap them there. Heh.
- The attacker is probably a machine.
- **Honeypots** create a virtual world to explore.
	- Many different options online
- Battle to discern real from the fake

#### Honeynets
A real network includes more than a single device.
- Servers, switches, routers, firewalls, workstations
- Keeps the attackers busy for longer with a larger deception netowrk

#### Honeyfiles
- Attract the attackers with MORE HONEY.
- Files with fake information that seems to be super important
- Send an alert if file is accessed.

#### Honeytokens
- Track the malicious actors
	- Add traceable data to the honeynet.
- API credentials
	- Notifs sent when used.
- Fake email addresses
	- Monitor the internet to see who posts them
