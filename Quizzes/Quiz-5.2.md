# Quiz 5.2 — Risk Management
*Professor Messer SY0-701 · Section 5.2*

---

### 1. A company performs a risk assessment as part of acquiring another company. What type of assessment is this?
- [ ] Continuous
- [ ] Recurring
- [ ] One-time
- [ ] Mandated

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> One-time</p>
<p>One-time assessments are tied to a single project, like an acquisition, new equipment installation, or a unique new security threat.</p>
</details>

---

### 2. Change control requires a risk assessment with every change. What type of assessment is this?
- [ ] Continuous
- [ ] Ad hoc
- [ ] One-time
- [ ] Qualitative

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> Continuous</p>
<p>Continuous assessments are part of an existing process.</p>
</details>

---

### 3. The CEO returns from a conference and asks if the organization is protected from a new attack type. A committee assesses that one attack, then disbands. What kind of assessment is this?
- [ ] Recurring
- [ ] Mandated
- [ ] Continuous
- [ ] Ad hoc

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> Ad hoc</p>
<p>Ad hoc assessments are done when the situation requires, often by organizations with no formal risk assessment process.</p>
</details>

---

### 4. An internal check is done every three months and the findings are logged. What type of assessment is this?
- [ ] Recurring
- [ ] Ad hoc
- [ ] One-time
- [ ] Quantitative

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> Recurring</p>
<p>Recurring assessments happen at standard intervals. Some are mandated by organizations or legal requirements.</p>
</details>

---

### 5. Which describes a qualitative risk assessment?
- [ ] Multiplying asset value by the exposure factor for each risk
- [ ] Gathering opinions on risk factors and showing them on a grid
- [ ] Dividing total uptime by the number of breakdowns for a system
- [ ] Calculating a yearly loss in dollars for every asset in use

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> Gathering opinions on risk factors and showing them on a grid</p>
<p>Qualitative assessments identify significant risk factors and often display them visually with a traffic light grid.</p>
</details>

---

### 6. What does the annualized rate of occurrence (ARO) measure?
- [ ] The percentage of an asset's value lost in one incident
- [ ] The total dollar value of an asset to the organization
- [ ] How likely an event is to happen in a single year
- [ ] The average time it takes to repair a failed system

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> How likely an event is to happen in a single year</p>
<p>For example, how likely is a hurricane to hit in Montana compared to Florida?</p>
</details>

---

### 7. When determining asset value (AV), what should be included?
- [ ] Only the original purchase price of the asset
- [ ] Only the cost to replace the asset with a new one
- [ ] The asset's cost, the effect on sales, and possible fines
- [ ] The number of times the asset failed in the past year

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> The asset's cost, the effect on sales, and possible fines</p>
<p>Asset value is the value of the asset to the organization, including potential regulatory fines.</p>
</details>

---

### 8. What is the exposure factor (EF)?
- [ ] The percentage of value lost due to an incident
- [ ] The number of incidents expected each year
- [ ] The dollar loss from a single event occurring
- [ ] The time between outages on a single system

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> The percentage of value lost due to an incident</p>
<p>A stolen laptop, for example, is a total loss, so its EF is 1.0 (100%).</p>
</details>

---

### 9. How is single loss expectancy (SLE) calculated?
- [ ] ARO x SLE
- [ ] AV x ARO
- [ ] EF x ARO
- [ ] AV x EF

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> AV x EF</p>
<p>SLE answers "what is the monetary loss if a single event occurs?"</p>
</details>

---

### 10. How is annualized loss expectancy (ALE) calculated?
- [ ] ARO x SLE
- [ ] AV x EF
- [ ] SLE x EF
- [ ] AV x ARO

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> ARO x SLE</p>
<p>For example, seven laptops stolen a year (ARO) x $1,000 (SLE) = $7,000 ALE.</p>
</details>

---

### 11. A server is worth $10,000, and a flood would destroy 25% of its value. What is the SLE?
- [ ] $250
- [ ] $2,500
- [ ] $25,000
- [ ] $7,500

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> $2,500</p>
<p>SLE = AV x EF = $10,000 x 0.25 = $2,500.</p>
</details>

---

### 12. Laptops worth $1,200 each are a total loss when stolen, and five are stolen per year. What is the ALE?
- [ ] $1,200
- [ ] $240
- [ ] $5,000
- [ ] $6,000

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> $6,000</p>
<p>SLE = $1,200 x 1.0 = $1,200. ALE = ARO x SLE = 5 x $1,200 = $6,000.</p>
</details>

---

### 13. Which impact is the most important consideration when assessing risk?
- [ ] Life
- [ ] Property
- [ ] Safety
- [ ] Finances

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> Life</p>
<p>Property is the risk to buildings and assets, and safety covers environments too dangerous to work in. Business impact can be more than monetary.</p>
</details>

---

### 14. How do risk likelihood and risk probability differ?
- [ ] Likelihood is quantitative, and probability is qualitative
- [ ] Likelihood is qualitative, and probability is quantitative
- [ ] Both are quantitative and are always calculated the same way
- [ ] Both are qualitative and are only rated rare or certain

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> Likelihood is qualitative, and probability is quantitative</p>
<p>Likelihood uses ratings like rare, possible, or certain, while probability is a statistical measurement. In casual conversation the two are often used interchangeably.</p>
</details>

---

### 15. A highway's posted speed limit, balancing safety and convenience, is an analogy for what?
- [ ] Risk tolerance
- [ ] Risk threshold
- [ ] Risk appetite
- [ ] Risk register

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> Risk appetite</p>
<p>Risk appetite is a broad description of acceptable risk-taking: the amount of risk accepted before taking any action to reduce it.</p>
</details>

---

### 16. Ticketing usually happens well above the posted speed limit, and that changes with road conditions and weather. This is an analogy for what?
- [ ] Risk appetite
- [ ] Risk tolerance
- [ ] Risk likelihood
- [ ] Risk transfer

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> Risk tolerance</p>
<p>Risk tolerance is an acceptable variance from the risk appetite.</p>
</details>

---

### 17. What is a risk register used for?
- [ ] Recording each vendor's due diligence and legal history
- [ ] Listing every user account and its group memberships
- [ ] Documenting each project step's risks and their solutions
- [ ] Tracking the average time between system breakdowns

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> Documenting each project step's risks and their solutions</p>
<p>Every project has a plan, but also has risk. The register applies possible solutions to the identified risks and monitors the results.</p>
</details>

---

### 18. In a risk register, who is assigned to manage each key risk indicator?
- [ ] A data processor
- [ ] A risk owner
- [ ] A data custodian
- [ ] A compliance officer

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> A risk owner</p>
<p>Key risk indicators identify risks that could impact the organization.</p>
</details>

---

### 19. What is the risk threshold?
- [ ] The point where mitigation costs at least as much as it gains
- [ ] The maximum number of risks a register can hold at one time
- [ ] The percentage of an asset's value lost in a single incident
- [ ] The amount of data loss that's acceptable after an outage

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> The point where mitigation costs at least as much as it gains</p>
<p>The risk threshold is where the cost of mitigation is at least equal to the value gained by mitigating.</p>
</details>

### 22. A company invests in new security systems to decrease its risk level. Which strategy is this?
- [ ] Mitigate
- [ ] Transfer
- [ ] Avoid
- [ ] Accept

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>

### 23. Which risk management strategy is often the usual course of action?

### 27. Which metric describes how long it takes to get back to a particular service level after an outage?

### 28. A database is restored, but it only has the last 12 months of data. Which metric describes how much data loss is acceptable?
- [ ] MTTR
- [ ] RTO
- [ ] RPO
- [ ] ARO

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> RPO</p>
<p>The recovery point objective defines how far back the data goes once the system is back online.</p>
</details>

---

### 29. What does mean time to repair (MTTR) include?
- [ ] Only the time spent replacing hardware
- [ ] Time spent diagnosing the problem too
- [ ] The time between one outage and the next
- [ ] The time to restore a year of backups

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> Time spent diagnosing the problem too</p>
<p>MTTR is the average time required to fix an issue. It's an important metric for determining the cost and time of unplanned outages.</p>
</details>

---

### 30. A system had 1,000 hours of total uptime and broke down 4 times. What is its MTBF?
- [ ] 4,000 hours
- [ ] 996 hours
- [ ] 250 hours
- [ ] 25 hours

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> 250 hours</p>
<p>MTBF = total uptime / number of breakdowns = 1,000 / 4 = 250 hours. It can be a prediction or calculated from historical performance.</p>
</details>
