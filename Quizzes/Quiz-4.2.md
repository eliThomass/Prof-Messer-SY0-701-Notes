### 1. Where does the acquisition/procurement process usually start?
- [ ] With a payment to the supplier
- [ ] With a request from the user, usually including budgeting info and formal approvals
- [ ] With an invoice
- [ ] With asset disposal

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> With a request from the user, usually including budgeting info and formal approvals</p>
<p>Procurement is a multi-step process: the user's request, negotiating terms and conditions with suppliers, then purchase, invoice, and payment.</p>
</details>

---

### 2. In asset accounting, what does "ownership" mean?
- [ ] The company that manufactured the asset
- [ ] Associating a person with an asset, which is useful for tracking a system
- [ ] Classifying the asset as hardware or software
- [ ] The asset's purchase price

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> Associating a person with an asset, which is useful for tracking a system</p>
<p>Accounting uses a central asset tracking system across the whole organization, recording ownership and classification (the type of asset, such as hardware or software).</p>
</details>

---

### 3. Why is it useful to associate a support ticket with a device's make and model?
- [ ] It replaces the need for an asset tag
- [ ] It can be more detailed than a user's description of the problem
- [ ] It's required for media sanitization
- [ ] It automatically fixes the issue

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> It can be more detailed than a user's description of the problem</p>
<p>Asset tracking means inventorying every asset — laptops, desktops, servers, and all associated hardware components.</p>
</details>

---

### 4. Listing all the parts of an asset, such as its CPU, memory, storage, keyboard, and mouse, is called what?
- [ ] Classification
- [ ] Enumeration
- [ ] Degaussing
- [ ] Procurement

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> Enumeration</p>
<p>Assets can also be given an asset tag, like a barcode, RFID, organization name, or tracking number.</p>
</details>

---

### 5. What makes media sanitization different from physical destruction?
- [ ] Sanitization removes all data so the storage media can be reused
- [ ] Sanitization always destroys the drive
- [ ] Sanitized data can be easily recovered with forensics tools
- [ ] Sanitization only applies to paper documents

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> Sanitization removes all data so the storage media can be reused</p>
<p>Media sanitization is a one-way trip with no recovery by forensics tools. It can clean a drive for future use, or delete single files or an entire system.</p>
</details>

---

### 6. What does degaussing do to a hard drive?
- [ ] Encrypts all data on the drive
- [ ] Removes the drive's magnetic field, making it unusable and unreadable
- [ ] Shreds the platters into small pieces
- [ ] Wipes the data while keeping the drive reusable

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> Removes the drive's magnetic field, making it unusable and unreadable</p>
<p>Physical destruction methods include a shredder or pulverizer (heavy machinery), a drill or hammer (quick and easy, through all the platters), and electromagnetic degaussing.</p>
</details>

---

### 7. When a third party destroys your data, what document confirms it was destroyed?
- [ ] A certificate of destruction
- [ ] A purchase order
- [ ] A data retention policy
- [ ] An asset tag

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> A certificate of destruction</p>
<p>A certificate of destruction provides a paper trail, so you know what happened to the data.</p>
</details>

### 8. Which two main factors drive how much data an organization retains?
- [ ] Regulatory compliance (required backups like emails and financial data) and operational needs (accidental deletion, disaster recovery)
- [ ] Employee preference and storage color
- [ ] Only the cost of storage
- [ ] Only the age of the data

<details>
<summary>Show Answer</summary>
<p><strong>Answer:</strong> Regulatory compliance (required backups like emails and financial data) and operational needs (accidental deletion, disaster recovery)</p>
<p>Data should be differentiated by type and application, so you can recover the right data when you need it.</p>
</details>
